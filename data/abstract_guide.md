AI Capstone Abstract Writing Guide

Purpose of this guide
This handout helps students write a clear, concise, and academically appropriate 250-word abstract for final
year projects in Artificial Intelligence. The abstract should communicate the project background, problem,
contribution, method, results or expected outcomes, and significance.

1. What is an Abstract?
An abstract is a short summary of your whole project. It helps readers quickly understand what your project is
about, why it matters, what you developed, how you evaluated it, and what contribution it makes.

For an i-FEST submission, your abstract should be academic but simple, specific, and easy for both technical and
non-technical readers to follow. The word limit of a research abstract is usually 250 words.

6. Complete Sample Abstract
Title: Explainable Deep Learning Prototype for Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

Word count: 250 words

Artificial intelligence has been increasingly used in healthcare to support early disease detection, improve screening
efficiency,  and  assist  clinical  decision-making,  especially  in  resource-limited  clinical  environments  and  settings.
Diabetic retinopathy is a vision-threatening complication of diabetes, and early identification is important to prevent
avoidable blindness.  However, manual  assessment  of  retinal fundus  images is  time-consuming, requires  trained
specialists,  and  may  delay  referrals  in  settings  with  high  screening  demand.  This  project  aims  to  develop  an
explainable deep learning prototype for classifying retinal images into normal and abnormal categories. The main
contribution of this project is the integration of automated image classification  with explainable AI visualization to
make  model  predictions  more  transparent  for  users.  The  methodology  involved  using  a  publicly  available  retinal
image  dataset,  resizing  and  enhancing  the  images,  and  training  a  convolutional  neural  network  for  binary
classification.  The  model  performance  was  evaluated  using  accuracy,  precision,  recall,  F1-score,  and  confusion
matrix  analysis.  Explainable  AI  heatmaps  were  also  generated  to  highlight  image  regions  that  influenced  the
classification decision. The prototype achieved an expected accuracy of above 85%, with recall prioritized to reduce
the risk of missing abnormal cases. The system allows users to upload a retinal image and receive a predicted class
together with a visual explanation of the model’s focus area. This project can support preliminary eye screening by
reducing  workload,  improving  consistency,  and  assisting  faster  referral  decisions.  Although  the  prototype  is  not
intended to replace clinical diagnosis, it provides a practical foundation for developing  accessible, low-cost, and
explainable AI tools for healthcare screening.

Keywords: Artificial Intelligence; Deep Learning; Diabetic Retinopathy; Retinal Image Classification; Explainable
AI; Medical Image Analysis; Healthcare Screening

7.  Final Student Checklist
•
Is the abstract close to 250 words?
•  Does the first part clearly introduce the background and problem?
•  Does the abstract state the project aim clearly?
•  Does it explain the novelty, contribution, or practical value?
•  Does it mention the AI method, dataset, system, and evaluation metrics?
•  Does it include actual results or expected outcomes?

8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake

How to Improve

Too much background

Keep the introduction short. The abstract should focus on the project, not a long
literature review.

No clear problem

Readers should understand what gap or challenge your project addresses.

Vague aim

Avoid phrases such as “This project is about AI.” Be specific about the task and system.

Method is missing

Mention the dataset, model, and evaluation briefly.

Overclaiming results

Do not claim clinical, industrial, or real-world effectiveness unless it has been properly
validated.

Too many technical details

Avoid long lists of parameters, code libraries, or implementation details unless essential.
